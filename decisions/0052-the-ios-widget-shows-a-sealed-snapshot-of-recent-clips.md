# 0052. The iPhone and iPad widget shows Pesto cards from a small sealed snapshot, kept only while a widget is installed; a tap copies in place or through the app

Status: Accepted (owner request, 2026-11-15); its "no History Lock and no Hide Clip Contents today" is superseded by ADR 0054, which keeps no snapshot while the lock is on

Extends ADR 0002 (sealed at rest), ADR 0003 (secrets are masked and shown on request), ADR 0041 (when a picture fills its card), ADR 0046 (Pesto on iPhone and iPad), ADR 0047 (the share extension's sealed inbox) and ADR 0051 (the AutoFill set, whose sealing this reuses). Nothing they decide is reversed.

## Context
A Home Screen widget is a separate process (`PestoWidget`, `net.copypesto.pesto.widget`) that WidgetKit starts whenever it wants a timeline, with Pesto not running. Giving it the store or the history key would put every clip's key into another process, as ADR 0047 and ADR 0051 refused for the other extensions. A widget also cannot read the app's preferences, so it cannot know the theme. A widget's `Link` and `widgetURL` open only the app that contains it; an interactive widget can run an App Intent in its own process; and iOS keeps a long press on a widget for its own menu (Edit Widget, Remove), so a widget has no long-press action.

## Decision
**Recent Clips**, kind `AppIdentity.widgetKind`, an `AppIntentConfiguration`: `systemSmall` (one card filling the widget), `systemMedium` (three cards in a row) and `systemLarge` (six in two columns), two and four at an accessibility text size (`WidgetSize`). No Lock Screen families, no Live Activity, no controls. Each clip is a Pesto card at widget scale on the theme's ground (carried in the snapshot): the card's white, or white at 10 percent on a dark ground, with its hairline and 12 pt corners; a color fills its card with its hex in the card's black or white ink (`CardInk`); a picture fills its card when its shape is near a card's (`CardTreatment.pictureFillRange`, the app card's rule, ADR 0041) and is otherwise shown whole over a blurred copy; a link leads with its host and its path small; text of 48 characters or fewer on one line is set large, longer text in whole lines; code in monospace; a file by name. One quiet foot line gives the source (the app, Another device, or how it came in on this device, ADR 0049) and the age as the card writes it (`CardAge.text`, moved by timeline entries at each minute or hour it changes, `WidgetContent.refreshDates`), or the age alone when the source does not fit. Clip content is `.privacySensitive()`, so iOS redacts it where the person asked it to (a locked device). No snapshot reads "Open Pesto to show your clips"; an empty list "No clips yet" (or "No pinned clips yet").

**The widget's settings** (`RecentClipsConfiguration`), per widget, no app setting:
- *When a Clip Is Chosen* (the owner's "when a clip is tapped"; the UI vocabulary avoids "tap"): **Copy** (default), **Open in Pesto**, **Open the Clip** (`WidgetTap`). Copy runs `CopyClipIntent` from a `Button(intent:)`, in the widget's process: it opens the snapshot and writes that clip's whole text (and, for a link that is one web address, the URL too) to the clipboard, without opening Pesto, and the tile says Copied for two seconds (`WidgetCopyRecord` keeps only the id and time, in the extension's own preferences). This was measured in the iOS 27.2 simulator: the text reached the system clipboard and pasted into Safari. It is offered only when the snapshot carries the clip's whole text: a text, code, link or color clip of at most 4 KB, while 32 KB of whole texts last. A longer clip and every picture are copied by opening Pesto (`pesto://clip/<UUID>`): the app copies through its one copy path and flashes the card. An in-place copy does not move the clip to the front of the history (Pesto is not running). Open in Pesto opens the clip's detail without copying (`…/show`). Open the Clip hands a link clip to the browser through the app (`…/open`), which acts only when the clip's whole text is one `http` or `https` address with a host (`WidgetTap.webURL`); anything else opens in Pesto.
- *Show*: **Recent** or **Pinned**. A collection is not offered: the snapshot does not carry collections, and choosing one would need the widget to read more than the snapshot.

**The snapshot** (`WidgetSnapshot`, chosen by `WidgetSnapshotSelection`): the newest eight clips, and separately the newest six pinned ones, that `ShortcutClipSearch.isOfferedOutsidePesto` allows (never a secret, a clip still being judged, one the content filter flagged, or one that expires), each as its id, kind, the first 200 characters of its preview, its whole text only as above, a link's host and path (120 characters each, no query or fragment, from `LinkDisplay`), a color's six hex digits, a source label (60 characters), its date and pin flag, and for a picture a JPEG thumbnail at most 240 pixels on its longer side and 24 KB (`ImageDownsampler.thumbnailJPEG`), at most 160 KB of thumbnails in all; plus the theme's light and dark ground and accent. It never holds a picture's or file's content, a text over 4 KB, a note, tags, a title the person typed, the collection, a file's folder, the source app's bundle id or anything of a covered clip.

**Sealing.** The format and key handling of ADR 0051 are now shared (`SealedFileFormat`, `SealedFileEnvelope`, `SealedGroupFile`, `SharedFileKeyStore`): the file is `widget-snapshot.pestowidget` in the app group, `PESTOWGT`, a version byte, then AES-GCM-256 over the JSON with the header and the purpose `pesto.widget-snapshot.v1` as associated data; written whole, atomically, with complete file protection; read with a 512 KB bound, which the fullest snapshot the bounds allow still fits. The key is a random AES-256 key of its own (account `AppIdentity.widgetKeyAccount`) in a Keychain group of its own, `$(AppIdentifierPrefix)net.copypesto.pesto.widget`, this device only, never synchronized, readable after first unlock. The widget's entitlements name that group alone, so it cannot read the quick-insert key and AutoFill cannot read the widget's; the app lists it third, after its own group and the quick-insert group. **This key opens only that file.**

**When it exists.** Only while at least one Pesto widget is on the person's Home Screen (`WidgetCenter.currentConfigurations`, asked when Pesto becomes active and when it leaves the foreground); with none there is no file and no key, and the Keychain is not touched. While a widget is installed the snapshot is rewritten a second after a save changes the history, one write at a time (so an older snapshot never lands after a newer one), and the app then reloads the widget's timelines. Clear History writes an empty snapshot at once. The iOS app has no History Lock and no Hide Clip Contents today (the Mac's cover is a screen-share defence; iOS has no such state), so there is no other state to empty it for; one added later must empty it too. The widget only reads the file, on each timeline request and in an in-place copy, and keeps nothing decrypted between them; any failure draws the unavailable state.

**The links.** The app registers the `pesto` scheme, as the Mac does, and acts only on `pesto://clip/<UUID>`, `…/show` and `…/open` (`WidgetLink`): the scheme, the host `clip`, a UUID, at most that one action word, and no query, fragment, user or port; anything else is ignored. Only a clip that is still eligible is copied, shown or opened; an unknown or no-longer-eligible id only opens the library. A link opened from elsewhere can do no more than the widget could.

## Consequences
- What sits outside the store while a widget is installed: up to 512 KB of the newest non-secret previews, whole short texts and small thumbnails, sealed, in the app group, and a key in a Keychain group the app and the widget share. Code signed by the same team could read both, as with the inbox and the AutoFill set; nothing else can.
- A clip deleted or made secret leaves the snapshot within about a second of the save while Pesto runs; the widget shows the old one until WidgetKit asks for a new timeline after the reload. If Pesto is killed in that second, the next foreground rewrites it.
- A widget removed while Pesto is away leaves the file and key until Pesto next becomes active or leaves the foreground.
- An in-place copy on a device is a hand check: the simulator showed it, and iOS may ask the person to allow pasting in the app that reads it, as for any copy.
- On a device: an App ID for `net.copypesto.pesto.widget` with the app group and Keychain Sharing, in its provisioning profile, and the main app's profile carrying the new Keychain group.
- A Mac widget is not part of this: it needs the Mac app's own signing decision (a Developer ID profile with an app group the sandboxed widget can share).
